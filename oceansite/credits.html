Carbon credits · restoration credits

A credit is a measurement with paperwork.

The market is short of units that can be pointed at. A lake has coordinates, a depth, a sediment and a monitoring record. What we sell is not an estimate of an avoided emission somewhere — it is a change at a named place, measured before and after by someone who is not us.

Carbon credits — where the tonnes come from

Oxygen-depleted sediment is an anaerobic environment. Anaerobic sediment generates methane, which leaves the water body and reaches the atmosphere. Restore oxygen to the deep zone and the conditions that produce methane are suppressed.

The mechanism

Methane formation in lake sediment is an oxygen-limited process. This is established limnology, not a proprietary claim, and not something OceanSite measured.

The quantity

What is creditable is the emission that did not occur, measured against the documented pre-intervention state of that specific water body.

The proof

Continuous sensor data plus sampled verification over the crediting period, attributed to an independent party.

The weakness we do not hide

Quantifying avoided methane at water-body scale is harder than measuring oxygen. Any buyer will ask how the tonnes are derived, and they are right to.

Restoration credits — the same project, a different buyer

A recovering water body is the unit that nature-restoration and biodiversity frameworks are designed to purchase. It is bought by different budgets, under different rules, and for different reasons than carbon.

What is credited

Documented ecological recovery of a defined water body against its own baseline — not an offset calculated elsewhere.

Why it is durable

The intervention is physical and on site. It can be inspected, re-measured and, if it stops working, seen to have stopped.

Who buys it

Obligation-driven buyers (permits, compensation requirements) and voluntary buyers with local or catchment-level commitments.

Stacking

Carbon and restoration units arise from one intervention. Whether both may be issued and sold, and to whom, is a rules question — see the open item below.

How a project runs

1. Baseline

The deep zone is characterised before anything is installed. This is the document every later claim is measured against, and it cannot be reconstructed afterwards.

2. Intervention

OxTube oxygenation sized for the site, with control and sensing supplied from within the group.

3. Monitoring period

Continuous dissolved oxygen and supporting parameters, running through the crediting period rather than sampled at its ends.

4. Verification and issuance

Independent attribution of the result, then issuance under the applicable methodology and registry.

What we do not claim

Rekilampi is oxygen data, not carbon data. Three years of near-bottom oxygen measurement demonstrates that the intervention works at depth. It is not a quantified tonnage and is not presented as one.
Ecological recovery is slower than oxygen recovery. Sediment nutrient release and full ecological response are multi-year processes. Oxygen responds first; the ecosystem follows, and the credit period has to reflect that rather than the other way round.

Measurement first, instrument second.

The baseline can start before the crediting route is settled — and starting in that order loses nothing, while starting in the other order loses the project.
