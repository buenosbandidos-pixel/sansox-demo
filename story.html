Our story — 2012 to today

It did not start with water treatment.

It started with hydropower turbines — and turned when oxygen proved the bigger idea. This is the company history as SansOx tells it, with the awkward parts left in.

Happihyrrä

The company began as Happihyrrä Oy, trying to make small hydropower turbines more efficient and climate-friendly. The economics did not carry. But the work surfaced something better: dissolving oxygen into water had far more untapped potential than turbines. The name became Sanso — Japanese for oxygen — and a registry requirement added the X. Some read it as French: sans + ox, "without oxygen". The product fixes exactly that.

The prototype that worked

Garden-hose fittings and all — the first OxTube removed iron from surface water in Kokkola: the first field proof of the four-stage process. Siuntio followed, a pumping station with two adjacent wells and different water chemistries treated in one line — iron precipitated in one, CO₂ stripped and pH lifted in the other, to the benefit of the local spa.

What iron removal actually looks like: oxidised iron precipitating out of treated water.

The awards years

The moment itself, on stage. [TARKISTETTAVA — names and year to be confirmed with SansOx]

Water Europe (WSSTP) named OxTube the Best Innovation in the European water industry in 2014. Best Aeration Company and the SaoxFuge innovation award followed in 2015; IroxFlotation was runner-up at Aquatech Amsterdam 2017. The lesson SansOx itself draws from those years: focus is essential — the company streamlined everything around OxTube.

Out into the world — and into a pandemic

A multinational client cut calcium from wastewater through CO₂ dissolution — reuse enabled, discharge costs down — until an acquisition shelved the rollout. Deliveries and pilots reached Saudi Arabia, Vietnam, Japan, Singapore and Dubai; the EU Gateway Programme opened the Philippines with Unique Water; a Korean joint venture was initiated. Then Covid-19 froze travel across Asia. The company's own history is unusually frank about this: progress was sometimes slower than hoped.

Back to nature

Years of projects led to a simple conclusion: water should not only be treated — it should be restored. Dubai's desert lakes and India's village ponds showed OxTube could revive stagnant water. That became the Nature Rejuvenation mission, reaching to the Baltic Sea — and it is why this site's structure follows the water cycle: natural, potable, used.
