Baltic Sea · April 29, 2024

With partners, a winner of this year's Baltic Sea Project

The City of Salo, THL, Savonia University of Applied Sciences and SansOx received funding to study removing pharmaceutical residues from discharge water with dissolved ozone.

The Baltic Sea Project, initiated by the Bank of Åland, encourages organisations to come up with fresh ideas for improving the well-being of the Baltic Sea, and funds the promising ones.

This year the consortium of the City of Salo, the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare, Savonia University of Applied Sciences and SansOx was one of the funded winners.

What the study looks at

The awarded project studies how pharmaceutical residues could be removed from discharge water by dissolving ozone into it, and how harmful microbes could be removed at the same time. There is a third dimension: because the ozone is dissolved through OxTube, which dissolves gas into water very efficiently, residual ozone stays minimal while the dissolved oxygen in the discharge water is high. The study also examines how that higher dissolved oxygen affects water quality around the discharge point.

The outcome will be results on all of the above, and academic articles as well.

Why it matters

It is important to start doing something about this: the countries around the Baltic Sea discharge on average 1.8 million kilograms of pharmaceutical residues into the sea. At the same time large areas of the Baltic suffer from a lack of dissolved oxygen. Raising the dissolved oxygen in discharge water with OxTube — without any extra energy — could improve water quality in some of those areas.
