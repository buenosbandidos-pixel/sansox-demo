Next step

One site. 60–90 days. Measured.

We do not ask a utility or an irrigation community to believe a brochure. We ask for one site and one defined outcome, and we measure whether it was reached.

What a pilot fixes in writing

Dose and reagent

What is fed into the water, and how much.

Contact conditions

Flow, pressure and residence time at the installation point.

Verification points

Where the water is measured, and against which limit.

Point of compliance

The single location at which success or failure is judged.

No commercial guarantee is given before that evidence exists. Pilot outcomes require site-specific replication — that is the reason for the pilot, not a disclaimer added afterwards.

What we need from the site

The water

Incoming quality data, flow range and the limit that must be reached.

The installation point

An existing pipe section, its dimension and available pressure.

The decision-maker

Who signs for a pilot, and what the internal approval path is.

The measuring party

Your laboratory, ours, or an independent institute — agreed before the first sample.

What we cannot send, and will not improvise. A typical oxygen transfer efficiency range now exists (SAE 4.0–6.0 kg O₂/kWh, company-reported — see home page); the site-specific figure is still calculated per site, not quoted as a guarantee. There is no independent third-party report on micropollutant removal; the 42-substance table is a manufacturer document. Anything a permit application has to rest on is generated in the pilot, with the measuring party agreed in advance.
