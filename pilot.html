Technical annex

The detail behind the promise on the front page: the process inside the tube, the energy question, the manufacturer’s reference values and a sizing tool.

Inside the tube: the four-stage process

Fast gas transfer and chemical reactions inside the existing water flow.

Separation

Dissolved substances are pulled apart from the water as it enters the tube. Air, oxygen, ozone or a selected reagent is fed or drawn into the vortex at the same point.

Activation

The separated molecules and the incoming gas are driven together in a zone designed so that they meet rather than pass each other.

Clarification

The target reaction happens here — the unwanted gas is stripped, or the target compound is broken down.

Replacement dissolving

What was removed is replaced: the surplus gas dissolves into the now-clarified water. All four stages happen in one pass, inside one tube.

Verify

Sensors and sampling confirm water quality and operating performance.

Compared with surface aeration: targeted delivery at depth, no rotating parts inside the OxTube unit, and suitable for under-ice operation when designed for the site.

Where the energy comes from

In an activated sludge plant, aeration is the single largest item on the electricity bill. So the first question a plant manager asks is not how well it works. It is what it costs to run.

The unit

Line pressure drives it

The process runs on the kinetic energy already present in the flowing line, which requires an inlet pressure of at least 2 bar. There are no moving parts inside the unit, which is also why service needs are low. Where site pressure is not sufficient and a pump is added to reach it, that pump's consumption belongs in the energy calculation, not outside it.

Company-reported
A reported treatment-plant case makes this concrete: an OxTube at the end of the discharge pipe aerated the treated outflow with air drawn from a clean location — external energy demand 0 kW; the water’s own kinetic energy did the work (company presentation 9/2024; site not named).

Ozone application

Only the generator draws power

Installed in a treated-discharge line, the unit itself adds no energy consumption — the electrical load is the ozone generator, not the dissolving step.

Reagent use

Low ozone consumption

Because the gas and the target molecules are forced to meet inside the tube rather than left to find each other, less reagent is needed. Overdosed ozone can be recycled back into the gas channel.

Without reagent

Air alone does some of the work

Manufacturer document
60 % reduction of MS2 — a virus used in testing as a stand-in for human viruses — was measured using air suction with no ozone at all. Not a substitute for disinfection — but it shows what the geometry alone contributes.

Company-reported Typical range now available; site-specific figure confirmed in pilot. The company reports a Specific Aeration Efficiency (SAE) of 4.0–6.0 kg O₂/kWh for the OxTube lake-oxygenation application — for comparison, surface aerators typically reach 0.9–2.1 and diffusers 0.6–7.0 kg O₂/kWh. In many installations the unit runs on existing line pressure, so no additional electricity is needed at all. The site-specific value is still confirmed against that plant's measured consumption during the pilot.
