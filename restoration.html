Independently measured Measured change: near-bottom oxygen reached 0.28–0.36 mg/L in 2026, compared with about 0.0–0.1 mg/L at baseline.

A deep zone holding no oxygen supports nothing living. The change measured here is small in absolute terms, and it is the difference between those two states.

A small, deep pond is the geometry where oxygen disappears near the bottom and stays gone: depth prevents natural mixing from reaching it. That is what makes Rekilampi worth citing — it is the hard case, not the easy one.

Carbon and restoration credits

Two separate instruments from one intervention: an avoided-emission credit and a documented ecological recovery. Different buyers, different rules.

Why a restored lake is a carbon question at all

A credit needs a mechanism before it needs a methodology, and the mechanism here is not obvious from the outside.

An oxygen-free bottom emits

Sediment that has run out of oxygen decomposes anaerobically and releases methane. The water body is then a source, not a store.

Oxygen suppresses the conditions that produce it

Restoring oxygen to the deep zone removes the conditions under which that decomposition happens. The avoided emission is what a carbon credit would be counted in.

SansOx lists this as an application in its own right

Manufacturer document “Transferring lakes from carbon source to carbon sink” appears on the manufacturer’s own list of OxTube applications. It is the company’s claim, not a measurement, and it is the reason this line of business exists.

Rekilampi did not measure this. Three years of dissolved oxygen data show the deep zone is no longer anoxic. They do not quantify avoided methane, because methane was not what was measured. Anyone converting the one into the other needs the methodology below — and we do not have it yet.

Until they are, what is delivered is the restoration and the verified evidence of it — which is also what any future credit would have to be built on.
