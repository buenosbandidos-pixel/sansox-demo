How the treatment works

Fast gas transfer and chemical reactions inside the existing water flow.

Separation

Dissolved substances are pulled apart from the water as it enters the tube. Air, oxygen, ozone or a selected reagent is fed or drawn into the vortex at the same point.

Activation

The separated molecules and the incoming gas are driven together in a zone designed so that they meet rather than pass each other.

Clarification

The target reaction happens here — the unwanted gas is stripped, or the target compound is broken down.

Replacement dissolving

What was removed is replaced: the surplus gas dissolves into the now-clarified water. All four stages happen in one pass, inside one tube.

Verify

Sensors and sampling confirm water quality and operating performance.

Compared with surface aeration: targeted delivery at depth, no rotating parts inside the OxTube unit, and suitable for under-ice operation when designed for the site.

Where the energy comes from

In an activated sludge plant, aeration is the single largest item on the electricity bill. So the first question a plant manager asks is not how well it works. It is what it costs to run.

The unit

The water drives it

The process runs on the kinetic energy already present in the flowing line. There are no moving parts inside the unit, which is also why service needs are low.

Ozone application

Only the generator draws power

Installed in a treated-discharge line, the unit itself adds no energy consumption — the electrical load is the ozone generator, not the dissolving step.

Reagent use

Low ozone consumption

Because the gas and the target molecules are forced to meet inside the tube rather than left to find each other, less reagent is needed. Overdosed ozone can be recycled back into the gas channel.

Without reagent

Air alone does some of the work

One group, one chain of evidence

A credit is only worth what the measurement behind it is worth, and a measurement is only worth what the treatment it describes actually did. These three steps are normally bought from three suppliers, and the evidence is handed over twice — each handover is where a buyer’s technical advisor starts asking questions. Here the three companies have the same owners.

Treat

SansOx Oy — this company — supplies the hardware in the flow: OxTube dissolving units, piping and pumps. This is the step that changes the water.

Measure

SpringNet Solutions Oy supplies the control software, the sensors and the continuous data. It records what changed, and it keeps the record.

Certify

OceanSite Oy turns a documented change into an instrument a buyer can hold: a carbon credit or a restoration credit.

What this chain does not yet deliver. Issuing a credit requires an approved methodology and a registry to issue it into. Neither has been selected. Until they are, the chain delivers treatment and the evidence of it — not issued credits. This is stated here rather than discovered by the buyer in the first technical meeting.
