Another pond to be saved — lake and pond aeration in action

A ClariOx unit was installed under the ice of a Finnish pond, one metre down, to raise dissolved oxygen through the winter without stirring up the sediment.

In Finland we live under the illusion that our natural waters are in good shape. Much of the time they are — but more and more ponds, and even lakes, suffer from problems that make the water quality poor.

Low dissolved oxygen is the symptom

One symptom of poor water quality is a low amount of dissolved oxygen. It usually happens in winter, when the ice cover stops air from dissolving into the water while consumption of oxygen carries on underneath. The other difficult season is summer, when the water warms and the temperature layers stop moving vertically. In summer the situation is easier to see; in winter everything happens under the ice, so nobody notices.

Installing ClariOx under the ice

Last week SansOx described the first project installed in one of Finland's many ponds. This time it was the turn of a pond where the ice cover takes over completely. Over the weekend a ClariOx system was installed under the ice. The pond is not large, but its deepest point is 15 metres and there is a great deal of solid material on the bottom.

ClariOx was placed about one metre below the ice, so that it raises dissolved oxygen substantially without mixing the sediment back into the water. The local University of Applied Sciences will take measurements through the winter and spring to see what effect raising oxygen under the ice has.

What it may mean for the fish

A couple of years ago a large part of this pond's fish population died during the summer. It will be interesting to see what raising the dissolved oxygen through the winter does for the fish next spring and summer.
