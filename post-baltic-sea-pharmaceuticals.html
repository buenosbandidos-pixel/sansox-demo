Baltic Sea · December 5, 2024

SansOx's solution keeps pharmaceutical residues out of the Baltic Sea

1.8 million kilograms of pharmaceutical residues reach the Baltic Sea every year. The technology to remove them exists; what has been missing is the obligation to use it.

The Bank of Åland's Baltic Sea Project funds solutions that improve the state of the Baltic Sea. Pharmaceutical residues are one of the growing problems, and the biggest difficulty is that although the problem is recognised, nothing is being done about it.

The EU now appears to be waking up, and hopefully pharmaceutical residues in the water leaving treatment plants will soon be measured and removed. The situation is that the technology exists — but does the will exist, as long as it is not compulsory? An estimated 1.8 million kilograms of pharmaceutical residues enter the Baltic Sea every year.

The Bank of Åland's magazine carried an article describing the SansOx solution to this, and to many other water problems that SansOx technology improves.
