Six pumping stations, one of the world's strictest radon limits — met.

The Philippines enforces a radon limit of 11 Bq/l for drinking water — far below the 300–400 Bq/l applied in parts of Europe. Together with our partner Unique Water, SansOx delivered radon removal units to municipal pumping stations.

1The starting point

Radon is a radioactive gas that seeps from bedrock into groundwater — invisible, odourless, and the second leading cause of lung cancer. Affected stations need continuous stripping; conventional aeration towers are large and maintenance-heavy.

An OxTube unit installed in a pump house — compact enough to stand in a corner. [TARKISTETTAVA — site and country to be confirmed with SansOx; a Philippines station photo is still requested from Unique Water]

2What was installed

RadOx — a combination of Booster, OxTube and GasRemox. Booster and OxTube strip radon and the radon–air mixture from the water; GasRemox separates the gas. Depending on the site, collected radon is contained or vented safely.

A technician at work on an installed unit — and the unit itself, flanges and flexible couplings visible. [TARKISTETTAVA — site to be confirmed]

4What follows from this

The same stripping principle applies to any dissolved-gas problem in potable water: radon, H₂S, CO₂, manganese and iron precursors. Existing pump stations can be upgraded without new basins — the unit installs in the line.
